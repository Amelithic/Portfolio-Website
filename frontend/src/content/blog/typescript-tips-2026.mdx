TypeScript rewards the small decisions you make consistently. Here are five that have stuck with me.

First, prefer `interface` for objects and `type` for unions. Second, use `satisfies` to check a value against a type without widening it. Third, keep `strict` on and let the compiler complain early.

Fourth, make impossible states impossible: model your data so that invalid combinations cannot be represented. Finally, let the framework infer your types wherever possible instead of annotating everything by hand.
